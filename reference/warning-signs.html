Warning signs

When to call us · after any procedure
Keep this with
your recovery guide

Most post-operative concerns are easy to resolve — sometimes with a quick phone call, sometimes on your own once you know what's normal. This page helps you tell the difference. When in doubt, call. We'd rather answer a quick question than have you worry at home.

Urgent — act right away Don't wait

Difficulty breathing or swallowing
Rapidly spreading swelling to your neck, under your tongue, or around your eye
Signs of severe allergic reaction: widespread hives, tongue swelling, wheezing
Chest pain or fainting

Call our office right away for the on-call provider. If you truly cannot breathe, or if chest pain is severe, call 911 or go to the ER.

Call our office Within hours

These apply after any oral surgery procedure:

1
Severe pain on day 3–5 that your medication doesn't help at all
2
Pain, swelling, or bad taste that returns after you'd started to feel better
3
Heavy bleeding that won't stop after 30–45 min of firm pressure
4
Swelling getting worse after day 3, or spreading beyond the surgical area
5
Fever over 101°F (38.3°C)
6
Numbness in your lip, chin, or tongue that isn't clearly improving by the next morning — call right away if it still feels completely frozen after the anesthetic should have worn off
💡 Is it dry socket?
If your medication brings pain down to a 2–3 out of 10, it's not dry socket. If the medication does nothing at all, that's the hallmark. Call us — easily treated.
💡 Is it an infection?
Watch the direction of your pain, not the amount. Each day should be better than the last. If you were improving and then it came back, call us.
Procedure-specific warning signs
After wisdom teeth Jaw stiffness getting worse (not better) after day 5
After an implant The implant feels loose or moves when you touch it
After a sinus procedure Air through your mouth when breathing through your nose, or liquid from your nose when you drink
After a gum graft The graft turns dark purple, blue, or black, or falls off
After sedation (first 24h) Severe confusion, trouble staying awake, or persistent vomiting
After a bone graft A large amount of graft material coming out, or membrane exposure getting worse

Normal — you don't need to call for:

These are all expected parts of healing and don't mean something is wrong.

Pink-tinged saliva for the first 24 hours
Swelling peaking at 48–72 hours, then improving
Mild bruising of the cheek, jaw, or neck
Yellow or whitish tissue in the socket (healing, not pus)
Slight bad breath or odd taste for a few days
Sutures dissolving or coming loose over 1–3 weeks
Jaw stiffness that slowly improves each day
A gum graft looking white or gray for 1–2 weeks
Slightly altered sensation that's gradually improving
A small metal healing cap visible at an implant site
For after-hours concerns, call our office and follow the instructions to reach the on-call provider. When in doubt, always call — we'd rather hear from you.
