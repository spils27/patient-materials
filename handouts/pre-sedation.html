You're scheduled for a procedure with IV sedation. A small IV in your arm will deliver medication that keeps you relaxed and comfortable. Most people don't remember much of the appointment afterward. This guide will help you prepare safely — the most important parts are on this page.

1 Eating & drinking

An empty stomach keeps you safe during sedation. The rule is eight hours — count back from your appointment time and stop then. Please follow this exactly.

When
What's allowed
Your last meal
Eat a normal, light meal before your eight-hour window starts. Avoid heavy, fatty, or fried foods.
8 hours before
Nothing to eat or drink. No food, no water, no coffee, no gum, no mints, no candy.
1 hour before
You may take your approved medications, and your triazolam if prescribed, with a small sip of water — no more than a teaspoon. This does not break your fast.
⚠
If you accidentally eat or drink inside your eight-hour window, call us before coming in. We may need to reschedule. This rule exists because food or liquid in your stomach can enter your lungs under sedation — rare, but serious.
ℹ
How to count your eight hours. For an 8:00 am appointment, that means nothing after midnight. For a 4:00 pm appointment, nothing after 8:00 am — you do not need to fast from midnight. If you're not sure, call us.

2 Medications & supplements

Follow these unless we gave you different directions at your consultation.

Take the morning of

  • Blood pressure medications — with a small sip of water
  • Heart, seizure, thyroid, and most daily prescriptions
  • Inhalers if you use them
  • If prescribed: your antibiotic and Peridex (chlorhexidine) rinse, started 2 days before surgery — take the antibiotic as directed and rinse morning and night
  • If prescribed: triazolam — swallow one tablet one hour before your appointment with a small sip of water. It eases nerves but is not required. If you forget it, that's okay: it will not affect your sedation and your surgery goes ahead as planned.

Hold the morning of

  • Diabetes medications — contact our office for instructions before your appointment; some (Jardiance, Farxiga, Invokana, Steglatro) must be stopped 3–4 days ahead
  • Any medication we specifically told you to stop

Stop 3–5 days before (if your prescriber approves): fish oil, vitamin E, garlic pills, ginkgo, turmeric/curcumin, bromelain, high-dose ginger. These can increase bleeding.

ℹ
Never stop prescription blood thinners on your own. If you take warfarin, Eliquis, Xarelto, Plavix, or similar, we'll coordinate with your prescribing doctor.
ℹ
Taking a GLP-1 medication such as Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, or Zepbound? Keep taking it as prescribed and tell our office before your appointment. Our office policy for all patients on these medications: clear liquids only (no solid food) for the 24 hours before your 8-hour fasting window begins.

3 The days before your appointment

  • No marijuana/cannabis in any form, and no smoking or vaping anything, for 72 hours before your appointment — this is our office policy for elective sedation, and it protects your airway and heart
  • No other recreational drugs for 72 hours before
  • No alcohol for 24 hours before — it interacts with sedation medications
  • Let us know right away if you develop a cold, cough, fever, sore throat, or flu
  • If there's any chance you could be pregnant, please tell us before we start

4 Your escort & the adult with you after

A responsible adult (18 or older) must come with you, stay, and take you home. This is required — we can't begin your sedation without it. It's the escort, not the car, that keeps you safe: riding home together in a taxi or rideshare is fine.

What your escort needs to do

  • Come inside with you at check-in
  • Stay in the office during your procedure — no exceptions
  • Take you directly home — driving you themselves, or riding with you in a taxi or rideshare
  • Stay with you for the rest of the day and overnight (24 hours) after discharge

What doesn't work

  • Uber, Lyft, taxi, or bus on your own
  • Walking or biking home
  • Anyone who only drops you off or only picks you up — your escort comes in with you, stays, and goes home with you
  • Driving yourself — not for 24 hours after sedation

5 What to wear & bring

Wear

  • Short sleeves, or sleeves that roll above the elbow (for the IV)
  • Loose, comfortable clothing
  • Flat, closed-toe shoes
  • Glasses instead of contact lenses

Skip or remove

  • Makeup, lipstick, lip balm
  • Nail polish or acrylics on at least one finger (we monitor oxygen through your fingertip)
  • Jewelry, especially piercings
  • Heavy perfume or cologne

Bring: photo ID, insurance card, a current list of your medications (with doses), and your escort.

6 After your procedure

Sedation medication stays in your body longer than you feel it. Even if you feel fine, your judgment and reflexes are not back to normal for a full day.

First 24 hours · Have an adult with you

  • Rest quietly at home
  • Someone must stay with you — don't be alone
  • Sip water and eat light foods as your stomach allows
  • Take your pain medication as directed
  • Follow the post-op instructions for your specific procedure

First 24 hours · Do not

  • Drive a car or operate machinery
  • Return to work, school, or exercise
  • Sign contracts or make important decisions
  • Drink alcohol, use cannabis, or take medications we didn't approve
  • Care for children or pets on your own
ℹ
It's completely normal to feel drowsy, slightly nauseated, or a bit wobbly for several hours. Many people don't remember parts of the day — this is expected and will not come back.
ℹ
Breastfeeding? You may resume nursing as soon as you are awake, stable, and alert — there is no need to pump and discard milk (American Society of Anesthesiologists & Academy of Breastfeeding Medicine guidance). For a newborn, preterm, or medically fragile infant, ask your pediatrician whether a brief 6–12 hour pause is prudent.

Call our office if…

  • You develop a cold, flu, fever, or sore throat before your appointment
  • You accidentally ate or drank inside the NPO window
  • Your escort arrangement falls through
  • You're unsure about any of your medications
  • You could be pregnant
  • After your procedure: heavy bleeding that won't stop, trouble breathing, chest pain, severe pain not controlled by medication, or fever over 101°F

After hours, call our office and follow the instructions to reach the on-call provider. If you truly cannot breathe or have chest pain, call 911.

We'd rather answer a question before your appointment than have you worry about it. If anything here is unclear — especially about medications, eating, or your escort — please call our office.
