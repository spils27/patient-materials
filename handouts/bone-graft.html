After your bone graft

Recovery Guide · Socket Preservation & Ridge Augmentation

A bone graft rebuilds or preserves the bone where a tooth was lost, so there is enough support for a future implant, bridge, or denture. The graft is made of small bone particles, usually covered by a thin membrane that holds them in place while your own bone grows through them. Nothing is locked in on day one — your main job for the first few weeks is to leave the site completely alone. If another handout in your packet gives a shorter time for the same rule, follow this guide. Here's what to expect and what to do.

Today
Rest. Gauze. Ice. Soft, cool foods. Don't touch the site.
Days 2–3
Swelling peaks. A few graft grains in your saliva is normal.
Weeks 1–2
Gums close over. Sutures dissolve.
Months 3–4+
Graft matures into your own bone. We plan the implant or restoration.

1 The first 24 hours

Protect the site

A blood clot forms around the graft and holds the particles in place while healing starts. Losing it early can cost you part of the graft.

  • No rinsing, swishing, or spitting for 24 hours — let saliva gently drain
  • No straws or sucking motions for 72 hours
  • Don't touch the site with your tongue or finger
  • Don't pull your lip or cheek back to look at it — that tugs on the stitches

Bleeding

Bite gently but steadily on gauze for 30–45 minutes. Oozing and pink saliva for the first day is normal.

💡
If bleeding restarts: fold a fresh gauze (or a damp black tea bag — the tannins help) over the site, bite steadily without checking for 30–45 minutes, and keep your head elevated.

Ice for swelling

  • 20 minutes on, 20 minutes off, for the first 24–48 hours
  • Swelling peaks at 48–72 hours, not the day of surgery
  • From day 3, switch to warm moist compresses
  • Bruising on the cheek or jaw is normal and can appear days later

Eat soft, cool foods — on the other side

Good choices

  • Yogurt, cottage cheese, pudding
  • Smoothies (spoon only — no straw)
  • Mashed potatoes, applesauce
  • Scrambled eggs, soft pasta
  • Lukewarm soup, broth

Avoid for at least a week

  • Hot or spicy foods for 72 hours (can restart bleeding)
  • Hard, crunchy, chewy foods
  • Chips, nuts, popcorn, rice, seeds — they lodge in the site
  • Carbonated drinks
  • Chewing on the graft side

Rest

No exercise, heavy lifting, or strenuous activity for 5 days — elevated blood pressure can restart bleeding. Sleep with your head elevated on an extra pillow the first few nights.

2 The rules that matter most

The graft particles take weeks to knit together. Until then, pressure, suction, and scrubbing can shift them.

  • No smoking, vaping, or smokeless tobacco for 1 month. Nicotine constricts blood vessels and slows bone healing — it is the habit most likely to make a graft fail.
  • Don't push on the graft with your tongue.
  • Don't brush directly over the graft site, and keep electric toothbrushes and Waterpiks away from it for about a month.
  • No vigorous rinsing for 7 days. Gentle rinses only — let the water fall out of your mouth.
  • Don't wear a denture, partial, or flipper over the site until we've adjusted it. Pressure can disturb the graft. (Exception: if you received an immediate denture today, leave it in for the first 24 hours as your denture guide says.)
  • No alcohol for 7 days.
ℹ
About the graft particles: you may see small whitish-gray grains at the site — that's graft material. Don't pick at them. A few grain-like bits in your saliva over the first few days is normal and expected; you don't need to save them.

3 Your membrane

A membrane covers the graft to hold it in place and keep gum tissue from growing into it. We use one of two kinds — we'll tell you which you have.

Collagen (dissolving)

  • Dissolves on its own over a few weeks
  • May look like a whitish film at the site
  • Don't pull at it, even if an edge lifts

PTFE (non-dissolving)

  • Looks like a white or gray patch; a small exposed edge is normal
  • We remove it at a short visit — usually about 2 weeks after surgery, sometimes 4
  • Don't pick at it or try to remove it yourself
💡
Sometimes the membrane comes untucked or even comes all the way out. Please give us a call if it does.
ℹ
Sinus lift? If your graft was placed under the sinus, you'll also have the Sinus Procedures sheet: no nose blowing, sneeze with your mouth open, no straws, no flying, for 2–3 weeks (stricter than the rules above). Follow it for the details.

4 Rinsing & hygiene

  • Peridex (chlorhexidine) — morning and night. Start 2 days before surgery as a gentle rinse. After surgery, switch to a soak for 1 week: hold a capful over the site for 1 minute, then let it fall out — don't swish. Skip the dose on the evening of surgery (no rinsing for 24 hours). Nothing to eat or drink for 30 minutes afterward.
  • Weeks 2 and 3: switch from the soak to a swab. Dip a cotton swab (Q-tip) in the Peridex until it's saturated and gently wipe it along the stitch line, morning and night. Don't scrub. This keeps the incision clean without staining the rest of your teeth — Peridex stains with longer use, and a swab only touches the spot that needs it.
  • Saltwater, starting tomorrow. Mix ½ tsp salt in 8 oz warm water. Rinse gently 3–4× daily, especially after meals, and let it fall out of your mouth.
  • Brush the rest of your teeth normally starting tomorrow with a soft manual brush. After day 3, you can clean gently near — but not on — the site.
  • Sutures dissolve on their own over 7–14 days. Don't pull, pick, or cut them. If non-dissolving sutures were placed, we remove them at your follow-up.

5 What's normal vs. what's not

Normal (expected)

  • A few graft grains in your saliva the first few days
  • Swelling peaking at days 2–3, then daily improvement
  • Whitish film or patch over the site (membrane or healing tissue)
  • Slight bad taste for a few days
  • Stitches loosening as they dissolve

Call our office

  • The membrane comes untucked or comes out
  • Pain or swelling getting worse after day 4, or returning after you'd improved
  • Pus, or a bad taste that comes back after improving
  • Bleeding that won't stop after 30–45 min of steady pressure
  • Fever over 101°F
  • Numbness or tingling in lip, chin, or tongue that isn't improving after 2 days

Urgent — don't wait

  • Difficulty breathing or swallowing
  • Rapidly spreading swelling to your neck, under your tongue, or around your eye

Call our office right away for the on-call provider. If you truly cannot breathe or swallow, call 911.

6 Healing time & follow-up

Your first follow-up visit is scheduled before you leave the office — timing depends on your case. For pain, a combination of ibuprofen + acetaminophen works as well as prescription opioids for most dental pain — see your Medication Guide. If you were given an antibiotic, finish the full course.

A socket graft takes about 3–4 months to mature into solid bone before an implant can be placed. Larger ridge grafts take longer — we'll give you your timeline at your follow-up.

Most of what protects your graft is simply leaving it alone. If something doesn't look or feel right, call us — a quick check is always easier than rebuilding a graft.
